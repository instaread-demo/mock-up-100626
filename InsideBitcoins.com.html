CFTC sends proposed prediction market event contract rules to the White House

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission has sent two draft rules on prediction market event contracts to the White House Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs for review, reportedly in the week ending October 2.

One proposal, filed under the docket number RIN 3038-AF82, would broaden the definition of a swap, the financial instrument category the CFTC regulates, to include event contracts. The draft appears on the RegInfo.gov regulatory agenda, the White House office’s public record of pending rulemakings. A second, separate draft examines whether the definition of a swap can be narrowed to exclude gaming-style products.

If approved, that second rule would bar exchanges registered with the CFTC as Designated Contract Markets from listing contracts on online gaming products such as blackjack and craps. Designated Contract Markets are the CFTC-regulated venues where prediction market operators such as Kalshi list their contracts.

The swap definition is the legal fault line in the dispute over prediction markets. Kalshi argues its event contracts, including contracts on sports results, are swaps regulated at the federal level, while states treat them as gambling subject to state licensing. The rulemaking follows a US Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit ruling in favor of Tennessee last week, which reversed a lower court decision and allowed the state to regulate prediction market sports contracts. Kalshi began offering event contracts in Tennessee in 2025, arguing the products met the standard for swaps.

The CFTC action also lands as Congress moves on the same products. The Senate passed the Protect College Sports Act, a bill from Texas Senator Ted Cruz, by a margin of 77-22 on the same Monday. The act would give athletic conferences and associations the power to restrict athletes who take part in sports betting or sports event contracts.

OIRA reviews draft federal regulations before publication. The CFTC submission has no stated timetable for clearing that review, and the review can change or reject the drafts.
