Even before it could complete 3 months of service, the gold trimmed 747-8 donated by the Qatar royal family has been grounded for cosmetic upgrades. The swanky jet already boasts lie-flat massage seats for reporters, decorative bookcases, and a grand staircase.

by Sayan Chakravarty

The Qatar-donated Boeing 747-8 that has served as Bridge Air Force One since appears to have stepped away from presidential duty for a new round of upgrades, with the lavish aircraft now undergoing cosmetic work to its interior. An administration source told ABC News that the aircraft is currently receiving “cosmetic upgrades,” although the White House has not confirmed whether the work also includes the more substantial security enhancements previously promised.


The development comes after the aircraft disappeared from recent presidential travel, with the older VC-25A Air Force One brought back into service for domestic trips. That sudden change has now provided the clearest indication yet that the $400 million Qatari gift may have finally entered the upgrade period that had been discussed for months.


The timing is particularly significant because the President had already said last month that the aircraft would be sent away in October to be “maxed out,” with increased security measures and other improvements expected to take about 60 days. He described the goal as making the aircraft “magnificent,” with the interior and engines receiving attention, while saying that maximizing its capabilities would allow it to operate in any territory.


ABC News has now confirmed only that cosmetic interior upgrades are underway, while an administration source did not specify whether security work is included. The White House has also not provided a completion date or said when the aircraft will return to presidential service.

From royal jet to presidential aircraft

Bridge Air Force One had already undergone a remarkably rapid transformation before entering presidential service on July 1, when it carried the President of the US from Joint Base Andrews to North Dakota on its first official flight as Air Force One. The aircraft had been donated by Qatar’s royal family and retained much of its former VIP configuration, with the US conversion deliberately leaving many of its permanent walls, rooms, and structural elements largely untouched. The Air Force said the conversion prioritized presidential mission requirements while making trade-offs involving less frequently used capabilities, allowing the aircraft to be prepared in roughly 10 months.

The additional upgrades were first announced in July, just 20 days after the aircraft entered presidential service, when the President said it would soon be sent away to be maxed out. At the time, the announcement followed questions surrounding the aircraft’s defensive capabilities, although he did not publicly detail what systems would be added.


The administration subsequently indicated that the aircraft was safe for presidential travel while additional enhancements were planned. The latest description of the work as cosmetic therefore leaves an important distinction between confirmed interior refurbishment and the separate security upgrades previously discussed.


The aircraft’s existing cabin is already closer to a flying luxury residence than a conventional airliner, with beige and tan furnishings, gold tones, dark wood accents and striped carpeting creating an unusually opulent atmosphere. Its press cabin features 14 lie-flat pods with lumbar support and massage functions, while journalists have also described generous legroom, wide aisles, refrigerated drink compartments, and fully reclining massage seats.


Much of the original Qatari royal cabin was retained, including its room arrangement, oversized seats, couches and decorative woodwork, while selected leather finishes and veneers were upgraded and presidential seals were incorporated. The aircraft also retained 10 restrooms, while Arabic exit signage and Qatari artwork were removed during the earlier conversion.

What “cosmetic upgrades” could mean

The phrase “cosmetic upgrades” is broad and does not amount to a published work order for the aircraft. In a conventional VIP cabin refurbishment, such work can include renewing leather and fabric upholstery, replacing carpets, refinishing wood veneers, restoring scratched or worn surfaces, and updating curtains, artwork, insignia, and other visible details. Lufthansa Technik, which performs VIP aircraft refurbishments, treats such visual work separately from deeper technical modifications involving entertainment systems, cabin controls, or major structural changes. There is currently no public evidence identifying which rooms, materials, or furnishings are being altered aboard Bridge Air Force One.


That distinction matters because the aircraft has already revealed a few practical cabin quirks despite receiving considerable praise for its comfort. New York Times reporter Maggie Haberman observed that the thick curtain separating the press area from the staff and Secret Service cabin could be awkward during turbulence, while she also described the press area as cold enough to require a blanket and later reported that the cabin could become extremely hot before departure.

The Wall Street Journal also reported that the aircraft had fewer refrigerators than the existing presidential jets, potentially requiring accompanying aircraft to carry some presidential food. None of those observations establish that the current cosmetic work is intended to address those issues, since upholstery, carpeting and decorative finishes would not by themselves solve temperature-control or refrigeration limitations.


The return of the old VC-25A adds another intriguing element to the story. After the Bridge aircraft had handled presidential travel since July, the President recently returned to the older Air Force One for trips including Tennessee, Chicago, Texas and Oklahoma, while the Qatari 747’s last documented flight was September 16 to North Carolina. That change naturally raised the question of whether the Bridge aircraft had finally been removed from service for its promised upgrades, although ABC News has not reported an official statement confirming when the aircraft entered the refurbishment process. Its recent history also includes a brief disruption in September, when an emergency slide accidentally deployed and delayed the planned trip to the Republican midterm convention in Dallas.


If the previously stated 60-day timeline remains unchanged, Bridge Air Force One could remain unavailable through much of November, potentially overlapping with the ASEAN Summit in the Philippines and the APEC Summit in China. The President has confirmed plans to attend APEC, but the administration has not said how the aircraft’s refurbishment schedule will affect presidential travel. For now, the most concrete development is that the once-secretive luxury interior is undergoing cosmetic work, while the scope of any deeper security modernization remains undisclosed.
