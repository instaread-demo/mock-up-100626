Nine Secures Multi-Year Athletics Australia Broadcast Deal

Published on: 4th October 2026 at 8:28 PM
Arvind Hickman
Share
5 Min Read

Nine has secured a seven-year broadcast partnership with Athletics Australia at the dawn of a golden era of athletics Down Under.

The agreement will make Nine the home of Australian athletics from 2027 to at least the end 2033, taking the sport beyond the Los Angeles 2028 and Brisbane 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games and into one of the most significant periods in Australian sporting history.

Nine will cover athletics events from the Maurie Plant Meet in Melbourne, to the Australian Athletics Championships and the Olympic and Paralympic selection events on the road to Brisbane 2032.

It will also mean that the voice of athletics, Seven’s iconic broadcaster Bruce McAvaney, will no longer call Australia’s medal winning moments.

Australian athletes have had a golden year on the world stage, with superstars including Olympic silver medalist Jessica Hull, rising middle distance runner Cameron Myers; established star pole vaulters and Olympic champion Nina Kennedy and Kurtis Marschall; and high jump rivals and world leaders Nicola Olyslagers and Eleanor Patterson.

Perhaps the most notable rising stars are the Aussie sprint trio Gout Gout, Torrie Lewis and Lachlan Kennedy; the latter recently clocked a 9.85 second 00m sprint at the Commonwealth Games, making him the fastest Aussie in history.

Nine said that the partnership , which aligns neatly with its Olympic and Paralympic broadcast deals, extends well beyond event coverage.

It will use its unparalleled reach and ‘Sofa to Street’ proposition to deepen fandom and tell the stories of Australian athletics and its athletes across Nine’s streaming, publishing and digital assets.

The partnership will also include a dedicated preview show ahead of each Australian Athletics Championships and a feature-length documentary in the 2028 and 2032Olympic years.

The agreement follows a competitive market process undertaken by Australian Athletics, with a clear ambition to secure more than a broadcaster.

“This is a landmark day for athletics in Australia,” Australian Athletics CEO Simon Hollingsworth said. “Our athletes are among the best in the world, and this partnership gives more Australians the opportunity to see them compete, get to know them and follow their journeys.

“With the Brisbane Olympics and Paralympics 2032 on the horizon, we wanted a partner that shared our ambition for the sport and was prepared to invest in building athletics over the long term.

“Nine is not simply broadcasting our events, they are backing our athletes, telling their stories and helping us build a bigger audience for athletics across Australia.”

Nine director of sport Brent Williams said: “Australian Athletics is entering a golden era and we are thrilled to be on board such an epic ride through to Brisbane 2032 and beyond. Australia already boasts some of the most exciting athletes on the planet, with a great promise of more to come. We look forward to the journey, telling their stories and sharing their magical moments with all of Australia across the screens of Nine.”

Australian Athletics president Jane Flemming, a former Commonwealth Games heptathlon and long jump champion, said the partnership would create opportunities across every level of the sport.

“We know our sport is something everyone relates to, running, jumping, walking and rolling, and now the entire community will get to connect to it in an ongoing and live nature,” she said.

“From our athletes competing on the world stage to the kids discovering athletics for the first time on a Saturday morning, visibility matters.

“Seeing our athletes competing in Australian stadiums, live on national television, gives young Australians heroes to follow and something to aspire to.”

Australian Athletics events announced so far for the 2027 Chemist Warehouse Summer Series and Australian Athletics domestic season, will be shown live and free across the the 9Network and 9Now.

February 13: Adelaide International, SA Athletics Stadium

February 27: Maurie Plant Meet, Lakeside Stadium

March 6: Sydney Track Classic, Sydney Olympic Park Athletic Stadium

March 20: Hobart Track Classic, Domain Athletics Centre

April 1-4: Australian Athletics Championships, Sydney Olympic Stadium

Fatty: A Life Unscripted did the numbers for Channel Nine last night, drawing a total TV reach of 1.56 million, an average audience of 447,000 and a BVOD average of 70,000.

The show charted the life and times of Paul Vautin.

Vautin first hit the rugby league scene in 1979 with the Manly Warringah Sea Eagles. He would go on to play 11 seasons with his beloved Northern Beaches club before he would finish his playing career with Eastern Suburbs. During this time he notched up 204 first grade games and as captain he won a premiership with Manly in 1987.

The proud Queenslander represented both his state in numerous ferocious Origin series as well as Australia.

After his playing career he even tried his hand in coaching — with the Maroons.

It was arguably after his Rugby League days where he really gained a mass following. He joined the dark side in 1992 when he was picked up by Chanel Nine’s Wide World of Sports. Here he would commentate the rugby league and co-host the hilarious Footy Show with Peter Sterling, and later Erin Molan. The show stopped airing in 2017.

Vautin retired from Nine on 1 December 2024, ending a 33-year broadcasting career.

Nine’s The Block was the top-rating entertainment show of the night, beating Fatty on total TV reach and handsomely beating the show in its average audience.

The Block recorded a reach of 1.87 million, an average of 1.02 million and a BVOD average of 229,000.

The Chase was Seven’s top-rating show, with a reach of 1.27 million, an average of 645,000 and a BVOD average of 79,000.

A repeat of The Dog House was 10’s top performer, with a reach of 649,000, an average of 306,000 and a BVOD average of 15,000.
